Ready when you are

Keep the thermometer in the green zone. Logs heat the stones slowly; water on hot stones in the zone scores a bonus. Over 212 °F is too hot.

Keys: L or Up arrow adds a log, W or Space throws water, P pauses. The best score is kept only in this browser.

How to play

  1. One round is 90 seconds. You score 10 points for every second the room is in the green zone, 167–194 °F (75–90 °C) in the game.
  2. Logs are slow. A log takes a while to heat the stones, and the stones take a while to heat the room. The firebox holds three logs.
  3. Water is quick. Throwing water on hot stones takes some heat out of them and makes steam. A throw while the room is in the zone is worth 25 points.
  4. Watch the door. Now and then someone comes in and the room cools a little.
  5. The round ends three ways. The clock runs out (100 bonus points), the fire dies and the room goes cold, or the room stays over 212 °F (100 °C) for 4 seconds and the stove overheats.

The zone is a game setting, not advice on how hot your sauna should be. The one real-world number is the top line: the Harvia wood stove manual says "a good rule of thumb is that temperatures of over 100 ºC are too high in a sauna" (Harvia manual, section 2.5, PDF). When the round ends, the game tells you what a real session does at that point: step out and cool off.

Not medical advice. A real stove is not a toy: follow your stove's manual for how much wood to burn, and never leave a fire unattended. If you feel unwell in a sauna, step out.
